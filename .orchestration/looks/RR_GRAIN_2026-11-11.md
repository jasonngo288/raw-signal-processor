# RR roll-stock grain re-tune after filmDevelop (2026-11-11)

Scope: `RollStock.grain(iso:tungsten:)` and the new `RollStock.grainScale(look:)` in `Sources/RawCore/RollStocks.swift`;
grain test in `Tests/RawCoreTests/RollStocksTests.swift`. No finisher change (grain shape, highlight fade, clamp untouched).

## Key finding: grain is constant, the Apple reference is not

Grain contribution on flat pixels, `sqrt(after^2 - before^2)` of the flat-texture std (log2 Y high-pass, σ 2 px), is
nearly the same on every file at a given stock (ISO 800: 0.063-0.080; 400: 0.054-0.068; 160: 0.037-0.045).
That is the roll-consistency we want. The *ratio* to Apple swings because Apple's own flat texture varies 3x between
files (BVQX ISO 32 sky 0.042, HDR01 Pixel 0.057, mooncake 0.070, IMG_1901 0.115, mug ISO 200 indoor 0.129).
So no constant grain keeps every frame in 0.9-1.3x. The table aims a typical frame (geometric-mean Apple texture
≈ 0.075) at ≈ 0.95x for ISO 100/160, ≈ 1.1x for 400 and ≈ 1.3x for 800. On clean frames like BVQX, the ratio sits above that.

Grain std per unit amount falls with size (0.186 at 0.055, 0.160 at 0.070, 0.144 at 0.080): a bigger cell puts less
energy in the σ 0.7-2 px band. So the 400 -> 800 step is mostly clump size, and amount has to rise with ISO too.

## Eye check (native 384² crops, 2x nearest-neighbour; Apple | old / new)

- BVQX sky: old chuan-400 was mottled and blotchy, like digital noise. The new one is a fine, even grain that reads as
  a lab scan. 800 stays clearly coarser, which is right for a night stock.
- mooncake cheek: old chuan-400 stippled the skin; new is gentle. da-mem-160 is pleasant, smooth but not waxy.
  dem-mem-800 is coarse sand on skin at 2x zoom; acceptable for "coarse grain, for low light" at 1x.
- HDR01 white wall: 400/800 show bright sparkle dots on near-white. That comes from the finisher (the grain fades to
  only 0.28 at Y 0.9, and the ±gain clamp is symmetric), not from the amount. Open for the finisher owner: a stronger
  highlight fade (film grain fuses in D-max) would make the whites cleaner.
- IMG_1901 faces / foliage and mug desk: grain is barely visible at all ISOs, because Apple's own texture there is high.

## 160 on mug (0.75x): why it was not raised

Mug's Apple texture (0.129) is mostly sensor noise and sharpening on an indoor ISO 200 frame. Reaching 0.9x would
need a grain std of ≈ 0.08 (the ISO 800 level), which puts the ISO 32 sky at ≈ 2.0x. A soft portrait 160 should
not do that. 0.75x on a noisy indoor frame is the honest cost of the softer decode. If it reads waxy to the user on device,
the lever is filmDevelop's sharp scale for portrait, not grain.
